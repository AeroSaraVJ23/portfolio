Networks that fly, and the cryptography that keeps them trustworthy.

I'm a Computer Engineering graduate currently working as a research intern at Griffith University, Australia, under Dr. Qinyi Li, on privacy-preserving post-quantum cryptography for 6G authentication networks and beyond — designing authentication schemes that stay secure once large-scale quantum computers arrive.

That interest in trust and reliability under stress runs through my earlier work too — from an AI-assisted routing framework for flying ad-hoc networks (FANETs) that used reinforcement learning to predict link stability, to forecasting anomalies in ISP-level BGP traffic, to SkyT, a cyber-physical precision-agriculture platform pairing UAV imagery with IoT soil sensors and cloud analytics.

I'm applying for PhD and Master's positions (research or coursework) in Computer or Electrical Engineering, with particular interest in post-quantum & applied cryptography, wireless/ad-hoc networking, and cyber-physical systems.
